# Pambianchi Masterclass 2026: Key Figures

Source: *Crafting Big, Bold Red Wines*, Daniel Pambianchi, August 26 2026, 37 slides. Captured in full 2026-09-18.

**The durable copy is the local file** `Wine Knowledge Base/Pambianchi Masterclass 2026 - Complete Slide Capture.md`, which carries every figure with its slide number. This is the working summary.

**Why the capture mattered.** The deck was browser-view only and access expires roughly one month after purchase. An earlier browser save produced blank pages 13 to 25. The complete PDF was recovered on 09-18, and slides 10 to 18, 22 to 25 and 29 to 37 are now captured for the first time.

## The question it settled

**Slide 9 is a four-stage SO2 timeline and its column headers are the timing:** AT CRUSH → **10 to 14 DAYS AFTER MLF** → AGING → AT BOTTLING.

So the first post-MLF addition is genuinely delayed 10 to 14 days. This contradicts Pambianchi's own Vino Superiore blog, where he wrote he would sulfite promptly, and contradicts Vinmetrica. **The deck governs**: it is more recent and more deliberate than a blog series narrating one batch.

Each wine is dosed 10 to 14 days after **its own** confirmed completion, on measured pH at 100% adjustment, then re-measured a week later and topped up 30 to 50% if short.

## Figures that change 2026 in flight

| | Slide | Figure |

| Filtering | 36 | **Target NTU < 2.0** |
| Bottling | 37 | Add **5 to 10 mg/L FSO2**, keep DO pickup below 1 mg/L |

## The 2027 findings

**Slide 29, the container table, points somewhere different from the barrel conversation.**

| | Oak | Oxygen | Complexity | Quality & aging |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Carboy | None | None | **Low** | **Low** |
| S/S + oak | Moderate | None | Moderate | Moderate |
| **HDPE + oak** | **High** | **Moderate** | **High** | **High** |
| Used barrel | High | Moderate | High | High |
| New oak barrel | Very high | Higher | Highest | Highest |

**HDPE plus oak adjuncts rates equal to a used barrel and above stainless plus oak**, because HDPE admits moderate oxygen where glass and steel admit none. A fraction of a barrel's cost, no Brett reservoir, no topping regime, scales to any volume. It was never considered and it is the best quality-per-dollar option on the table.

The deck also rates **carboys lowest on both complexity and aging potential**, and describes them as short-term storage.

**Where 2026 diverged from the deck's own numbers:**

| | Deck target | RR-2026 actual |
|---|---|---|
| Must pH | 3.3 to 3.6 (slide 5) | **4.10 both lots** |
| Nitrogen, 2nd dose | 1/3 sugar depletion (slide 17) | **57% and 68% depletion** |
| Total YAN | 300 to 350 mg N/L (slide 17) | not measured |
| Fermentation temp | **78 to 86°F** (slide 18) | pitched at **60°F**, peaked 77 to 80 |
| Cold soak | 3 to 7 days at **40°F** (slide 15) | 47 to 50°F |
| Punchdowns | taper **4× / 3× / 2×** (slide 19) | flat 2 to 3× |

**Untried levers appropriate to a hot, high-pH vintage:** amelioration, a 10 to 15% juice bleed at crush (slide 11), and cofermentation with 5 to 10% Viognier, Riesling or Gewürztraminer for copigmentation (slide 12).

**Next instruments, per slide 7's tier list:** VA, then NTU, then DO. ORP is "next level" and ranks below all three.
