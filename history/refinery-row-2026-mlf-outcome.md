## The Sangiovese has a measured acetaldehyde problem

**This is the finding of the vintage so far.**

On 10-12 the free SO2 titration consumed **1.0 mL on the Sangiovese against 4.49 mL on the Cabernet**, despite the Sangiovese having been dosed with 70 ppm the day before and the Cabernet with 43 ppm two weeks earlier.

Roughly **50 to 65 ppm of the 10-11 addition vanished inside 24 hours.** That is binding, not loss. Acetaldehyde binds SO2 one to one by moles, 1.45 mg SO2 per 1 mg acetaldehyde, so the implied load is **35 to 45 mg/L**.

It corroborates the tasting: nutty, bruised, and no mid-palate. The cause is in the record: this wine was bulk-degassed in error on 09-08 and then carried **zero SO2 from crush until 10-11**, six weeks at pH 3.8 and above, at 70 to 72°F.

Not a disaster. Bound acetaldehyde is sensorially silent and SO2 is the correct treatment, which is now in. But it needs watching rather than assuming.

## The SO2 target was wrong, and the references corrected it

**Pambianchi masterclass slide 9, AGING stage, sets molecular SO2 by vessel:**

- **Barrel, HDPE, VCT: 0.5 to 0.6 mg/L, check monthly**
- **Glass and stainless: 0.2 to 0.5 mg/L, check every 3 months**

Glass admits no oxygen, so it needs less. Both wines are in glass. The 0.5 molecular target used through 09-27 and 10-11 is the **barrel** number.

**Consequence: the Cabernet needed nothing on 10-12.** At 22 to 30 ppm free and pH 3.74 it is 0.26 to 0.35 molecular, mid-band for glass. No addition was made.

**And a competing target that is not met.** `Competition Targets - Acid and pH for Award Wines.md` carries the AWRI figure that **0.6 mg/L molecular is required to prevent Brettanomyces**, which at pH 3.96 is **85 ppm free** on the Sangiovese. Reaching it needs about 4.5 g more, pushes total SO2 past 200 ppm, and lands in the range judges flag as excess sulfur dioxide. That file predicted this trap before it happened.

**Accepted for ten days** because no wood has ever touched this wine and glass carries no Brett reservoir, so inoculum risk is low right now in a way it will not be later.

**Which makes oak a hard constraint, not a preference.** Oak stays out of the Sangiovese until pH and SO2 are both settled.

## The titration is still not trustworthy

Three runs, none usable, all for the same reason: **the sample volume was never recorded.**

| Run | Titrant | At 25 mL (20×) | At 100 mL (5×) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cab, first | 6.3 mL | 126 ppm, impossible | 31.5 ppm |
| Cab, second | 4.49 mL | 90 ppm, impossible | 22.5 ppm |
| Sangiovese | 1.0 mL | 20 ppm | 5 ppm |

The 25 mL reading is impossible on the Cabernet two ways: 1.85 g KMS is 43 ppm at full retention from a zero base, and the wine completed MLF, which O. oeni will not do at 90 ppm free. **At 100 mL both Cabernet runs land inside the independent calculated estimate of 20 to 28 ppm**, which is why that is the working figure.

Note also that **the cream of tartar check validated the pH electrode, not the sulfite electrode.** They are separate sensors and the sulfite one has never been checked.

## Return plan, 2026-10-23, in order

1. **Free and total SO2 on both wines. Write the sample volume down.** Total minus free is the bound fraction, which is the real measure of the Sangiovese acetaldehyde load.
2. **Resolve the instrument**: sample volume the procedure actually calls for, titrant normality and date, and the condition of the SC-300 sulfite electrode.
3. **Rack the Cabernet without aeration** (0.2 to 2 mg/L O2 versus 3 to 8 with), then oak it. Needs an empty vessel and an MLF-complete topping wine, neither of which exists yet.
4. **Sangiovese acid bench trial**, with **blending modelled against tartaric**. Blending the Cabernet in lowers pH without raising TA at all, and the two wines are now further apart in pH, 3.74 against 3.96, which makes the lever better than when it was first written.
5. **Re-taste both** for the bruised note once SO2 has settled and bound.
6. **Oak, both wines**, after 3 and 4. Track **days on wood**, not calendar dates, so the French-versus-American comparison survives different start dates. A second MoreBeer OAK597 infuser is still needed.
7. **Re-measure TA on both after cold stabilisation.**
