## The three findings that shaped this vintage

**1. It was a potassium vintage and the vendor published it in advance.** Oak Barrel lists Brix, pH and TA for every lot in August. The 2026 sheet showed Sangiovese at **pH 4.16 / TA 6.0 / 23 Brix (Tehama County)** with the note "needs tartaric acid to reach 7.5 TA," and Cabernet at **pH 3.94 / TA 5.2 / 23.5 Brix (Suisun Valley)** with the same caution. Of six reds offered, Nebbiolo from Tehama came in at pH 3.75 / TA 7.2 and needed nothing. The must analysis the notebook lists as next year's top lesson already exists and is free.

**2. The competition frame reversed the acid recommendation upward.** Top-typicality Sangiovese measures pH 3.42 / TA 5.18 (Sacchi et al.), so you cannot acidify toward award numbers. But on the Davis 20-point scale total acidity is worth 2 points and "slightly high" costs 1, while aroma/bouquet and general quality are 8 points and that is where faults land. AWRI requires 0.6 mg/L molecular SO2 to stop Brett: 74 ppm free at pH 3.90 versus 52 ppm at 3.74. Lower pH also shifts anthocyanins toward the coloured flavylium form, worth 4 more points in clarity and colour. The acid buys fault-avoidance, not points. This moved the Sangiovese from 20 g to 30 g.

**3. Chaptalization spent the Sangiovese's MLF headroom, and that drove the strain split.** OG 1.105 (post-chaptalization) to FG 1.001 is 13.65 percent ABV at x131.25 and 14.04 percent at x135. The two Brix of sugar added to cover a shortfall against the vendor's published number is what made this MLF marginal. The Cabernet, at 12.86 to 13.23 percent, has a full point of headroom.

## Strain split, decided 09-09 and re-grounded 09-10

**Lalvin MBR VP41 on the Sangiovese. Viniflora Oenos 2.0 on the Cabernet.**

| | RR-2026-SAN-01 | RR-2026-CAB-01 |
|---|---|---|
| Strain | **Lalvin MBR VP41** | **Viniflora Oenos 2.0** |
| Rated alcohol | 16% (Lallemand TDS) | 14% datasheet / Pambianchi, "up to 15%" on the Novonesis live page |
| Wine alcohol | 13.65 to 14.04% | 12.86 to 13.23% |
| Min pH | 3.1 | 3.1 datasheet / 3.2 Pambianchi |
| Diacetyl | **Low** | **Medium** |
| Restart a stuck MLF | Rated for it | No |
| As pitched 09-10 | Direct addition | Direct addition |

**The case for VP41 is flavor, not survival, and that should be said plainly.** The Oenos alcohol ceiling is not settled: the Eaton/Novonesis datasheet and Pambianchi both say 14 percent, while the Novonesis live product page says up to 15. On the 15 reading the Sangiovese is comfortably in spec, so the survival argument is weaker than it looked on 09-09. What stands on its own is diacetyl. Diacetyl is butter, it is not a Sangiovese note, and the Sacchi panel found judges rate fresh and fruity wines as more typical of the variety. VP41's stated sensory impact is "Fruity, Mouthfeel, Freshness," its cinnamyl esterase activity is 0, and it produces no biogenic amines, which was the original reason Oenos was chosen. Nothing is given up.

**Choosing low diacetyl costs no mouthfeel.** MLF softening comes from the malic-to-lactic conversion and from polysaccharide production. Diacetyl is an aroma on a separate axis.

**Oenos belongs on the Cabernet and not as a consolation.** Medium diacetyl into American oak, alongside coconut, dill and vanilla, is stylistically consistent. Splitting the strains also removes the partial-sachet problem: neither carboy gets a half-used, resealed culture.

**Dose has no weighed answer at this scale.** The smallest freeze-dried sachets sold are 66-gallon (Lallemand 2.5 g, Viniflora 1.5 g), so a 6.5 gal carboy receives roughly 10x the label rate. That is unavoidable, and both manufacturers warn only against under-dosing.

## Corrections to earlier guidance in this project

- **The 9.0 g/L TTB line applies to the FINISHED wine.** 27 CFR 24.182 caps acid additions at 9.0 g/L fixed acid as tartaric in the finished wine, not at any transient pre-MLF value. Earlier entries here treated a pre-MLF TA above 9.0 as a wall, which over-constrained several decisions. The Sangiovese's 9.56 pre-MLF lands at 7.75 finished and is compliant with room.
- **Opti'Malo Plus is 20 g/hL, so 4.92 g per 6.5 gal carboy**, not the 1 g/gal (6.5 g) written in the original plan.
- **The Opti'Malo Plus window was never read off the label until 09-10, and a free head start was lost.** Lallemand's product page states the nutrient may be added "anytime from 48 hours prior to or up until the same time as the malolactic bacteria culture addition." It could have gone in on 09-09 at no cost and no risk. That was a planning miss, not a fault in the wine. **The window is a window, not a recommendation to wait**: Scott Labs says just before, and delaying inoculation to manufacture a gap would have extended the unprotected zero-SO2 period, which is the actual standing risk on these wines.
- **MLF temperature is 68 to 72F on both carboys**, not the 72 to 77F written for the Sangiovese on 09-09. That figure was taken from the top of the Oenos range on the theory that heat compensates for alcohol, and it was over-specified on a thin basis. The VP41 TDS gives 63 to 77F for reds but **64 to 72F under limiting conditions**, and at 13.65 to 14.04 percent this wine is limiting conditions. Pambianchi independently caps favorable MLF at 64 to 73F. Two sources arrived at separately, both capping at 72 to 73F. Higher temperature also drives redox potential down, so running hot has a cost beyond the bacteria.
- **First SO2 goes in 10 to 14 days AFTER MLF completes**, not immediately on a clean malic reading (Pambianchi masterclass, slide 9). Dose to target on pH at 100 percent, re-measure free SO2 one week later, adjust 30 to 50 percent if needed. Larger and less frequent beats smaller and more frequent.
- **Free SO2 targets revised down because these wines are in glass.** Pambianchi's aging spec is 0.2 to 0.5 mg/L molecular for glass and stainless, versus 0.5 to 0.6 for barrel. The AWRI 0.6 figure used earlier is the Brett threshold and is the right number for cooperage, not for glass. **Target 0.5 molecular: about 43 ppm free on the Sangiovese and about 45 ppm on the Cabernet.** **Both figures are PROVISIONAL.** They were computed from projected post-MLF pH of 3.74 and 3.76, which were themselves projected from pre-inoculation numbers now in question. Recompute against a measured post-MLF pH. Never carry these forward as settled.
- **Pambianchi rates Oenos diacetyl MEDIUM, not low.** An earlier entry claimed Oenos would give toasty and nutty rather than buttery character on the strength of a general Scott Labs note about diacetyl metabolism. That was an overreach. Retracted.
- **The VP41 spec sheet does not make rehydration mandatory.** An earlier entry said direct addition was wrong. Both methods are sanctioned by the TDS.
- **The SC-55 consumable that runs out is Biopressure Agent, not vials.** An earlier entry here said to order a vial refill. Vials are reusable, septa last about 3 tests, and Biopressure Agent covers roughly 10 tests. Order the agent.
- **The two broken member PDFs will not be fixed by re-saving.** Earlier advice here said to re-save them with print-to-PDF. That is unlikely to work: the content is served as page images for browser viewing only, with downloads disabled, which is why the saves came out blank in the first place.

## Remaining sequence

1. **Settle the calibration question**, then re-read both wines if the meter was not calibrated. Nothing downstream is trustworthy until this is closed.
2. **Hold both carboys at 68 to 72F.** Measured 73.8F (Cabernet) and 73.9F (Sangiovese) during the 09-09 heat event; both need to come down a couple of degrees.
3. **Malic at DAY 7 on both**, not week 3, because alcohol on the Sangiovese sits near the ML ceiling. On the Sangiovese continue **every 2 to 4 days** thereafter per the VP41 TDS. Confirm Biopressure Agent stock before the day 7 run.
4. **No SO2 on either wine until malic reads below 0.05 g/L**, and then not for a further 10 to 14 days.
5. **First SO2 dosed against the MEASURED post-MLF pH** for 0.5 mg/L molecular, roughly 43 and 45 ppm free on current projections. Re-measure one week later, adjust 30 to 50 percent. Expect ORP to drop sharply on this addition; that is normal.
6. **Oak goes in only after MLF is confirmed complete and the first SO2 is in.** French cubes in the Sangiovese at 40 g, American in the Cabernet at 45 g, both roughly half of the 91 g reference full dose (3.70 g/L). Dose both at the same time and at the same proportion of reference, or wood origin stops being the only variable. Taste at 4 weeks, then every 2. **A second MoreBeer OAK597 infuser is needed** (16 in, 2 oz / 57 g capacity) so both carboys can be oaked simultaneously.
7. **Re-measure Cabernet pH after MLF and decide on further acid then.** If it lands at 3.65 or below, cold stabilization starts lowering pH instead of raising it. Forcing that now would take ~22 g and finish at TA 7.60, but it would be a bet against a plus or minus 0.05 estimate of the MLF pH rise.
8. Re-measure TA after cold stabilization. KHT precipitation will pull it down further.
9. Free SO2 every 3 months through bulk aging in glass (Pambianchi's glass and stainless cadence; monthly is the barrel figure). Expect 4 to 8 ppm/month loss. Top up after every sampling.
