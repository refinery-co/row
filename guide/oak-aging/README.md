# Oak and Aging

---

## Oak Format

| Format | Extraction rate | Control | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barrel (used) | Slow — oxygen ingress drives integration | Low — hard to remove | High-volume production, consistent programs |
| Oak cubes | Medium — more surface area than barrel | High — removable, dosable | Home winemaking, 5–10 gal batches |
| Oak chips | Fast — maximum surface area | High | Pre-bottling adjustment, quick trials |
| Infuser / bag | Medium | High | Equivalent to cubes in a permeable bag |

For 6.5-gallon carboys, **cubes are the right format** — controllable, removable, dosable.

---

## Dose

Reference full dose: **91 g per 24.64 L (3.70 g/L)**.

Planned doses for 2026:
- Cabernet: 45 g American oak cubes
- Sangiovese: 40 g French oak cubes (once pH and SO2 are settled)

Pre-bottling chips: 2–4 g/L (Pambianchi slide 35).

---

## American vs French

| | American | French |
|---|---|---|
| Flavor | Vanilla, coconut, dill — pronounced | Spice, toast, more subtle |
| Tannin | Coarser grain, faster tannin extraction | Finer grain, slower extraction |
| Match | Cabernet, Zinfandel, Chardonnay | Pinot, Sangiovese, Burgundian varieties |

---

## Timing: Days on Wood, Not Calendar Dates

Track oak contact in days, not calendar dates. If two carboys get oak on different start dates, tracking calendar days makes comparison impossible. Days on wood is the universal comparison unit.

Taste weekly for the first month, then every two weeks. Remove when the oak character is where you want it — it integrates further in bottle.

---

## When Oak Can Go In

Oak is not a first step. Prerequisites:
- MLF complete (malic below 0.05 g/L)
- SO2 dosed and confirmed in range
- pH settled (not mid-correction)
- Wine racked off gross lees

**The Sangiovese constraint:** oak stays out until pH and SO2 are settled. Wood introduces oxygen and raises the stakes for a wine already carrying an acetaldehyde load.

---

## Topping Up

Headspace is the enemy of wine in glass. After racking and oaking, top up with a wine that:
- Has completed MLF
- Is at a compatible pH
- Is from the same or a neutral variety

A second small carboy or a few bottles of compatible wine solve the topping problem. Plan for it before racking.
