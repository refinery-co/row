# Grapes

Quality at the carboy starts at the vineyard. Everything downstream — corrections, MLF, SO2 management — is easier when the fruit arrives in range.

---

## The Lot Sheet

Read the lot sheet before ordering, not after. Every reputable retailer publishes Brix, pH, and TA for each lot. These three numbers determine the work ahead.

| Number | What it tells you | Action threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **Brix** | Sugar content, determines potential ABV | Below 22: may need chaptalization. Above 26: high alcohol risk, consider water addition. |
| **pH** | Acidity balance, microbial stability | Above 3.6 at crush: plan acid additions before fermentation. Above 3.8: significant correction needed. Above 4.0: high-potassium fruit — tartaric alone may not solve it. |
| **TA** | Total acidity as tartaric g/L | Below 5 g/L: acid deficient. Above 9 g/L: may need deacidification. Target for reds: 6.0–7.5 g/L finished. |

**The 2026 lesson:** both Suisun Valley Cabernet and Tehama Sangiovese arrived at pH 4.1. Oak Barrel Winecraft published the lot data in August. Reading it then changes the whole plan — strain selection, acid budget, MLF timing.

---

## High-pH Fruit

High pH (above 3.8) together with normal or high TA is usually a potassium problem, not simple acid deficiency. The two look similar on paper but respond differently.

- **Acid deficiency:** low TA, low-to-normal pH. Tartaric addition raises TA and drops pH predictably.
- **High potassium:** high pH despite adequate TA. Tartaric raises TA but barely moves pH because potassium tartrate buffers the solution. The right tool is KHT precipitation (cold stabilization), not more acid.

The AWRI pH crossover for KHT is **3.65**: above it, cold stabilization raises pH while TA falls; below it, stabilization lowers pH while TA falls. Know which side you're on before adding acid.

## ABV Planning

Estimate ABV range before fermentation:

`ABV = (OG − FG) × 131.25` (low bound) to `× 135` (high bound)

If chaptalization is needed: target OG, not sugar weight. Calculate how many grams of sugar per litre to reach the target, then multiply by litres.

Pick the MLF strain **before** chaptalizing — some strains have hard alcohol ceilings (Oenos 2.0: 14–15%, VP41: 16%). Adding sugar after the strain is selected can push you past its ceiling.

---

## 100 lb Lot Checklist

Before pickup:
- [ ] Read lot sheet: Brix, pH, TA recorded
- [ ] ABV range calculated — chaptalization needed?
- [ ] MLF strain selected against projected alcohol
- [ ] Acid addition estimated from lot pH and TA
- [ ] Yeast selected
- [ ] SO2 at crush planned (if pH above 3.4: none until MLF is confirmed safe)
