# Primary Fermentation

From crush to dry. The goal is a clean, complete fermentation that doesn't create problems for MLF and aging.

---

## Yeast Selection

| Strain | Character | Alcohol ceiling | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| BDX | Structured, full extraction, low volatile acidity | 15% | Cabernet, Merlot, Syrah |
| BM 4X4 | Fruit-forward, complex, cold-tolerant | 16% | Sangiovese, Italian varieties |
| EC-1118 | Neutral, robust, restarts sluggish ferments | 18% | Mead, stuck ferment rescue |
| 71B-1122 | Fruit ester production, malic acid reduction | 14% | Mead, light reds, rosé |

Select yeast before chaptalizing. Chaptalizing past the yeast's alcohol ceiling stalls fermentation.

---

## Chaptalization

Add sugar when Brix is below 22 and target ABV requires it.

`grams sugar needed = target OG × volume in litres × (target OG − 1) × 1000 × 0.0038`

Simpler working rule: 17 g/L sucrose raises Brix by approximately 1°.

**Timing:** add at 1/3 sugar depletion, not at crush. Early addition stresses yeast. Add dissolved in a small volume of must, not dry.

---

## Nitrogen and Nutrients

Yeast needs nitrogen (YAN) to complete fermentation without producing off-flavors.

- Add nutrient at **1/3 sugar depletion** — not at pitch.
- Warm must to **65°F before pitching** — cold must stresses yeast from the start.
- DAP (diammonium phosphate) is effective but adds ammonium smell if overdosed. Fermaid-O or Fermaid-K are more complex sources.

---

## Temperature Management

| Phase | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Pitch | 65°F minimum | Cold must stresses yeast, risks sluggish start |
| Active fermentation | 65–75°F | Cooler = more fruit retention, slower extraction |
| Extended maceration | 65–70°F | After dry, before press |

---

## Press Timing

Press at or near dry (SG below 1.000). Extended maceration (leaving wine on skins post-dry) extracts additional tannin and color — useful for structured varieties, potentially excessive for lighter ones.

Measure SG before press. Do not press on taste alone.
