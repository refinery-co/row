# Acid Corrections

Acid corrections set the foundation for MLF, SO2 efficacy, and long-term stability. The goal is not the lowest pH — it is the right TA and pH combination for the wine you are making.

---

## TA vs pH: Which Drives the Decision

pH and TA measure different things and do not always move together.

- **TA (titratable acidity):** total acid load — what you taste as tartness. Target for reds: 6.0–7.5 g/L (Boulton 1999).
- **pH:** the active hydrogen ion concentration — drives microbial stability, SO2 efficacy, and color. Target for red wine: 3.3–3.6. AWRI: adjust to 3.4 or below regardless of tartaric required.

**TA sizes the acid addition. pH is the check.**

Use the landing equation to calculate the dose. Then verify the resulting pH against the target. If pH is in range and TA is in range, you are done. If one is in range and the other is not, something else is happening — usually potassium buffering.

---

## The Landing Equation

`post-MLF TA = starting TA + tartaric added − (0.56 × malic acid g/L)`

Rearranged to find the tartaric dose:

`tartaric dose (g/L) = target TA − starting TA + (0.56 × malic)`

`grams tartaric = dose (g/L) × volume (L)`

Example: 24.64 L Sangiovese, starting TA 4.0 g/L, malic 3.23 g/L, target post-MLF TA 7.0 g/L:
- `dose = 7.0 − 4.0 + (0.56 × 3.23) = 3.0 + 1.81 = 4.81 g/L`
- `grams = 4.81 × 24.64 = 118.5 g`

Measure malic before calculating. The landing equation is only as accurate as the malic number.

## When Tartaric Is the Wrong Tool

High pH + high TA = potassium problem, not acid deficiency. In this state, tartaric raises TA without moving pH because potassium tartrate buffers the solution — the worst of both outcomes.

Signatures of high-potassium fruit:
- Must pH above 3.8 on delivery
- Large tartaric additions move TA significantly but pH barely
- Fruit from warm, inland growing regions (common in California Central Valley, Suisun Valley)

The remedy is KHT precipitation through cold stabilization, not more tartaric. The KHT crossover pH is **3.65** (AWRI): above it, cold stabilization raises pH while TA falls; below it, stabilization lowers pH while TA falls.

## Bench Trials

Bench trials calculate the dose before committing to the full carboy. They are not optional on high-pH or high-buffering fruit.

**Procedure (Pambianchi, Conducting TA-pH Trials v1.0):**
1. Take a measured sample of must (100 mL typical)
2. Prepare a tartaric stock solution: 10 g tartaric per 100 mL distilled water (10 g/L)
3. Add measured volumes of tartaric stock to separate sample cups
4. Measure TA and pH in each cup after 30 minutes
5. Plot the response — pH vs addition, TA vs addition
6. Add **half the calculated amount** to the carboy, re-measure, then adjust

**The carboy is slower than the bench trial.** A pH reading 30 minutes after addition may read 0.15–0.22 higher than the equilibrated overnight reading. Wait overnight before deciding to add more.

**Do not taste bench trial samples** — tasting changes the sample volume and skews results. Taste only the Decision sample at the end.

---

## pH Response to Tartaric

Rule of thumb: approximately 0.1 pH drop per 1 g/L tartaric. This weakens in high-TA or high-potassium wine.

Measured in 2026:
- Cabernet: 0.123–0.14 pH/g/L — linear, responds better than the rule
- Sangiovese: 0.106 pH/g/L average with a flat shoulder at 0.03–0.04 through the 2.0–3.0 g/L range — buffering behavior consistent with high potassium

---

## Staged Addition

Three independent sources — Pambianchi (TA-pH Trials), Pambianchi (Amelioration Calculator), and Vinmetrica — all recommend adding half the calculated dose, measuring, then adjusting. Must volume is an estimate. Buffering capacity changes as acid is added. Split every significant addition.

---

## Regulatory Limits (reference only)

| Authority | Limit |
|---|---|
| OIV (Resolution OENO 14/2001) | 4 g/L tartaric, must and wine combined |
| EU, wine | 2.5 g/L |
| US TTB | No per-addition g/L cap; 9.0 g/L total fixed acidity in finished wine |

These apply to commercial producers. Useful as sanity checks.
